# Cognitive load and accessibility

## Evidence-backed guidance

- Use signaling to identify the active card or relevant phrase. Source: [multimedia research](../../raw/sources/multimedia-learning-2026-08-21.md#sources)
- Segment complex explanations into meaningful chapters. Source: [multimedia research](../../raw/sources/multimedia-learning-2026-08-21.md#sources)
- Remove decorative elements that compete with the learning goal. Source: [multimedia research](../../raw/sources/multimedia-learning-2026-08-21.md#sources)
- Place related labels and visuals near each other in both space and time. Source: [CTML capture](../../raw/sources/multimedia-learning-2026-08-21.md#sources)
- Provide a reduced-motion alternative for the interactive storyboard and avoid non-essential large parallax movement. Source: [W3C capture](../../raw/sources/motion-accessibility-and-korean-text-2026-08-21.md#sources)

## Korean text working constraint

Use Netflix’s 16-character line limit as a conservative test preset, not a universal law; normally keep subtitle cards to one line and never exceed two without a reason. Source: [Netflix capture and limitation](../../raw/sources/motion-accessibility-and-korean-text-2026-08-21.md#sources)

## Required audience test

After a 30-second animatic, ask viewers to state the problem, identify the source type, and distinguish confirmed facts from unresolved links. This is a project test proposal, not a claim from the cited research.
