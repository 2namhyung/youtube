# Spatial camera grammar

## Definition

A single large board acts as the story world. Camera movement explains containment, sequence, or relationship rather than replacing ordinary cuts with decorative zooms. The approach is inferred from spatial-scale and open-canvas references. Source: [visual references](../../raw/sources/visual-reference-works-2026-08-21.md#spatial-canvas-and-drawing)

## Rules

1. Use the loop `overview → detail → overview`.
2. Keep a stable anchor: board mini-map, timeline, axis, or selected-card marker.
3. One movement explains one relation.
4. Avoid rotation unless orientation itself is meaningful.
5. Move, settle, then reveal fine text.
6. Duration changes with travel distance and scene complexity rather than following one preset. Source: [Material motion guidance](../../raw/sources/motion-accessibility-and-korean-text-2026-08-21.md#sources)

## Open questions

- The safe zoom velocity for a television, phone, and desktop viewer remains untested.
- The current 1500×900 storyboard needs a camera-path model before it can export an animatic.
