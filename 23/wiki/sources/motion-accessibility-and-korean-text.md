# Motion accessibility and Korean text

- Source: [web capture](../../raw/sources/motion-accessibility-and-korean-text-2026-08-21.md)
- Captured: 2026-08-21
- Status: reviewed

## Claims and evidence

- Unnecessary motion may cause serious discomfort for motion-sensitive users, and interactive experiences should support reduced motion. Source: [W3C capture](../../raw/sources/motion-accessibility-and-korean-text-2026-08-21.md#sources)
- Netflix’s Korean timed-text guide uses 16 characters per line, normally one line and at most two, for the covered workflows. Source: [Netflix capture](../../raw/sources/motion-accessibility-and-korean-text-2026-08-21.md#sources)
- Motion duration should vary with distance and change rather than use one duration for everything. Source: [Material Design capture](../../raw/sources/motion-accessibility-and-korean-text-2026-08-21.md#sources)

## Limitations

The Netflix rules are production constraints, not a universal cognitive threshold. The W3C rule directly governs interactive content; its use for rendered video is a conservative design choice.
