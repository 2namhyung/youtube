Evidence: [2 fps full clip](ref/canonical-hanbok-10s-2fps.jpg), [1 fps overview](ref/canonical-1fps.jpg), [6 fps ending, 8.4–10.0 s](ref/canonical-ending-fine.jpg).

## Verdict

**PASS for a ~9.5–10.0 s shared walking master.** The clip supplies a continuous-looking centered walk with repeated alternating-foot steps and ongoing skirt/hand motion. It is materially safer than the earlier 4 s source for the shared card section.

## Observations

- Apparent framing is stable: head top, shoulder line, and overall body scale stay at essentially the same screen position across the 0–10 s overview. Small x/y changes are pose/stride changes, not visible camera zoom or scale drift.
- The walk remains active through the ending. In the 8.4–10.0 s strip the feet continue alternating, skirt hem swings, and the prop hand shifts; there is no multi-frame freeze or deliberate ending pause. The last pose is still mid-walk, so it can cut under a card transition without looking like a stop.
- The clip is not a claim of an infinitely seamless loop: no loop boundary was tested or needed here, and the final pose is not presented as a matched restart pose. Use the native 10 s take once for the shared master; avoid hard-looping it beyond its duration.
- Suitability is therefore strong for a card section lasting up to about 9.5 s of person performance. If the final composition needs the full 13.33 s reference runtime, extend only with a deliberate masked hold/pose or another longer canonical take; do not silently repeat frame 0 after 10.005 s.

## Motion character

The upper body is intentionally restrained and reusable: arms/props hang and swing naturally, gaze/head angle varies modestly, and the walking cycle supplies enough phase diversity for independent card overlays. City-specific upper-body actions should still be composited separately where required; this file is a stable shared hanbok walking base, not a replacement for those action tracks.
