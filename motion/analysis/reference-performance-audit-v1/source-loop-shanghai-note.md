# Source walk loop and Shanghai performance probe

Compared `production/ticket-travel/motion-edit-probe/source-hanbok.mp4` with `production/ticket-travel/performance-v2/shanghai-fan.mp4`.

- Both are 720×1280, 24 fps, 96 frames, approximately 4.0 s. Evidence: [source 4 fps](ref/source-4fps.jpg), [source 10 s repeated preview](ref/source-loop-10s.jpg), [source seam frames](ref/source-seam-frames.jpg), [Shanghai 4 fps](ref/shanghai-4fps.jpg), and the supplied [Shanghai contact](../../../production/ticket-travel/performance-v2/shanghai-contact.jpg).
- The source is a short staged walk/pose phrase, not a demonstrated seamless gait cycle. Rough phase read: 0.0–1.25 s advances through a broad step while the bouquet/pouch hand moves upward; 1.5–3.0 s settles into mostly front-facing, small alternating steps; 3.25–4.0 s starts another arm/step variation. The final frame and first frame differ in head direction, arm/prop placement, skirt/foot phase, and hand position. A hard repeat at 4.01 s will therefore create a visible pose/velocity discontinuity. The 10 s repeated preview is evidence of repeated phases, not proof of a seamless loop.
- Reusing this exact 4 s source to fill 9.5 s would require about 2.37 repeats. It can provide enough pixels for a rough assembly, but should not be labeled continuous or seamless. Safer options are a longer canonical walk, selecting/cutting only compatible phase windows with a brief masked transition, or holding/reframing selected settled poses between card reveals.
- Shanghai has broadly the same centered full-body walking scale and alternating-foot stride, so it is a usable broad-phase reference. Its upper body is materially different: from ~0.0–1.75 s both arms hang low while she carries a folding fan in one hand and a small handbag in the other; around ~2.0 s she raises/opens the fan across the chest and keeps it there through ~4.0 s. Head/gaze is mostly forward or slightly screen-left, with a calm/pleasant expression. Do not map Shanghai as a direct upper-body continuation of the hanbok source.
- Shanghai's fan raise is a useful city-specific action track, but the hand/prop and arm phase should be independently composited from the legs/body stride. The broad walking phase can align; upperbody action cannot be assumed to align frame-for-frame.
