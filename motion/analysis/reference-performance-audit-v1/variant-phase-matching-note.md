# 10 s outfit variant phase-matching QC

Compared each variant with `canonical-hanbok-10s.mp4` at the requested source intervals. Evidence strips: [canonical 3–5.5](ref/variants/canonical-3-5.5.jpg) / [Tokyo 3–5.5](ref/variants/tokyo-3-5.5.jpg), [canonical 5.5–7.5](ref/variants/canonical-5.5-7.5.jpg) / [Shanghai 5.5–7.5](ref/variants/shanghai-5.5-7.5.jpg), [canonical 7.5–9.5](ref/variants/canonical-7.5-9.5.jpg) / [Hanoi 7.5–9.5](ref/variants/hanoi-7.5-9.5.jpg), plus [full variant overviews](ref/variants/canonical.jpg).

These are visible phase/pose checks, not absolute joint certification.

- **Tokyo parasol, 3.0–5.5 s:** variant holds the large parasol overhead, giving the upper silhouette and arm placement a materially different shape from canonical's free hands. Across the five sampled times, the broad foot-contact phase appears compatible; this check did not quantify joint coordinates or establish exact frame-time identity. Use the Tokyo clip as its own performance track, with the parasol silhouette accounted for in card masking.
- **Shanghai fan, 5.5–7.5 s:** broad centered framing and walking scale remain close. Around 5.5–6.5 s the fan rises from low side position toward chest/face; by 7.5 s it is again a prominent side/chest prop. Canonical has ordinary alternating-foot walk through this interval. Upper-body/prop phase is intentionally different, while lower-body phase is close enough for a shared-master composite with a soft matte.
- **Hanoi hat, 7.5–9.5 s:** broad scale is close, but this interval contains a clear action change: fan/hat starts held in front of the torso, rises to cover/roof the head around 8.0–8.5 s, then stays overhead. Canonical continues a restrained hanbok walk. Hat lift changes the head/shoulder silhouette and temporarily occludes the face; treat as independent upper-body action and avoid a hard body cut at the lift.
- **Intro casual handoff:** [intro end 3.5–4.0](ref/variants/intro-end.jpg) ends with the casual traveler smiling, looking screen-right, legs crossed in a planted walking pose. [canonical start 0–0.5](ref/variants/canonical-start.jpg) begins looking down-left while holding the colorful prop, with a different foot/arm phase. Framing is broadly compatible, but direct frame-time continuity is visibly mismatched; the planned bottom-up card/person wipe should hide this rather than relying on a clean pose match.

## Recommendation

All three 10 s variants are usable as independent city performances over the shared section, with stable framing. Do not splice them by assuming canonical frame-time identity. Preserve each variant's own upper-body action and use the card matte/overlap to absorb the modest foot and pose-phase differences, especially Tokyo's parasol and Hanoi's hat lift.
